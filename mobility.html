MARKET STRATEGY / AUTONOMOUS MOBILITY   /   CASE STUDY 01

Where should autonomous mobility go next?

A market expansion decision system across 35 U.S. metros, with uncertainty brought into the conversation.

ROLE INDEPENDENT ANALYSTTOOLKIT Python · Snowflake · dbt · Power BIFOCUS Market readiness & strategy
01 / THE BIG QUESTION

A big market isn't necessarily a good market.

Autonomous ride-hailing and delivery are expanding, but choosing where to launch is not simply a question of population. Demand, weather, economics, infrastructure, competition and law all shape where a service can realistically succeed.

My question: Which U.S. metropolitan markets appear most attractive for robotaxi, sidewalk robot and drone delivery — and how much do the answers change when our assumptions change?

02 / THE APPROACH

From fragmented public data to a decision system.

I collected and transformed public datasets on population, employment, weather, the built environment and aviation, then combined them with manually curated regulatory evidence. Snowflake served as the warehouse, dbt as the tested transformation layer, Python handled sensitivity analysis, and Power BI made the results explorable.

The model deliberately separates baseline scores, robust rankings and assumption-dependent rankings. Markets without sufficient regulatory evidence are left unranked rather than incorrectly treated as poor candidates.

03 / WHAT SURPRISED ME

The most interesting finding wasn't always who ranked first.

01

San Francisco: a strong market, not an empty one.

San Francisco led baseline robotaxi readiness at 91.8/100, with Los Angeles and San Diego also in the leading group. But incumbent driverless operations change the question from “should we enter?” to “how would we compete or partner?”

02

Boston: one legal question, three different answers.

Boston was unranked in the baseline due to incomplete state-law evidence. Under pessimistic, neutral and optimistic legal assumptions, it placed 20th, 8th and 4th. The next useful action is legal due diligence — not an investment decision.

03

Pittsburgh: drone pilot candidate, not a guaranteed launch.

Pittsburgh ranked first for baseline drone readiness and stayed in the top ten in 95.5% of tested weight draws. It warrants pilot feasibility work, including current operator activity, airspace and partner verification.

05 / SO WHAT?
STRATEGY IMPLICATION

A ranking is a starting point, not a go/no-go decision.

Established robotaxi leaders call for competitive positioning or partnerships. Uncertain markets like Boston need legal verification first. Drone opportunities like Pittsburgh merit pilot feasibility work — not promises of commercial success.

Independent research using public information, not a live deployment system. Regulatory classifications are analytical assumptions, not legal advice. The underlying evidence snapshot was frozen as of October 5, 2026.
